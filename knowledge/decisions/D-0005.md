# D-0005 — The collective lever and the stick's tilt

Rusty (2026-10-10) asked for a throttle like Immersive Aircraft's. Read from its 1.4.6 jar
(`AirplaneEntity.updateController`): Up and Down move an engine target by 0.1 a tick, clamped 0 to
1, and it stays where left; the engine spools to it; a gauge over the hotbar. Its quadrocopter is
binary, as Rotorcraft was: Space climbed at the climb rate, Shift descended, let go held the height.
On the proposal Rusty answered "up and down only, and tilting forward or back more influences your
forward and back speed", and was told the design below; then "Build the batch".

**Decision**:
- **The collective is a lever** (`Flight.collective`, 0 bottom to 1 top) that Space and Left Shift
  move and that stays where it is let go. In its **detent** at the middle (`Flight.HOVER`, a tenth
  of the travel wide) it holds the height; above, it climbs at the share of the climb rate its
  distance from the detent is of the way to the top, and below likewise descends
  (`Flight.climbShare`). A full sweep takes a second; the lever moves a quarter as fast through the
  detent, so a held key is felt to pass it and a press of about two thirds of a second brings it
  from the top into the detent. A pilot taking the controls finds it in the detent
  (`Aircraft.takeTheWheel`), so boarding never lurches. The descent cap near the floor, the
  autorotation and the crash rules are unchanged: held all the way down, it still lands softly.
- **The stick tilts** (`Flight.stick`, -1 back to 1 forward) the longer W or S is held, full in a
  second and a half; the speed is aimed at the stick's share of the top (or reverse) speed, and the
  body leans with the stick. Let go, it springs back level and brakes to a hover, as before; held
  the other way it brakes at once.
- **A dial**, built as Immersive Aircraft's engine dial is and drawn where it draws it, centred over
  the hotbar (`client/CollectiveGauge`, frames from `devtools/art/gauge.py`): a round housing on a
  pedestal with the rotor turning inside it as the real one turns, and an arc lit orange from its
  bottom-left end up to the lever, the detent a white tick at twelve o'clock. Rusty, on a first
  gauge (a slim bar left of the hotbar): "the gauge looks like shit and its in a weird spot. I like
  the circular throttle bar from immersive aircraft more"; of the dial lit as a throttle and lit
  from the tick (green up, amber down) he chose the throttle. Only the pilot's client knows the
  lever, so only the pilot sees it.
- **The feel is the pilot's, in a client config** (`client/FlightFeel`, `config/rotorcraft-client.toml`,
  saved live from the Mods screen): the sweep time, the detent's width and slowdown, the tilt and
  spring times, and whether the stick stays where it is let go, as the lever does
  (`domain/Controls`). The pilot's client flies the aircraft and the server bounds every reported
  move by the airframe's rates, so no setting flies faster, climbs faster or turns harder; nothing
  new crosses the network and the network version is unchanged.

**Found in the build: a stick that springs back hardly changes the speed.** Both aircraft reach top
speed on their engine's acceleration in about 2.4 seconds (the Huey 1.4 at 0.03 a tick, the
Chinook 1.2 at 0.025), so a stick that tilts fully in 1.5 seconds never holds the speed back, and
let go it brakes as before: only the lean changes. The speed is graded by the tilt only if the
tilt is slower than the acceleration, or if the stick stays where it is let go, so the pilot can
cruise at part speed. Both are a config setting away; the playtest on the 4070 settles which.

**Why**: the lever is what Rusty asked for, after the mod he named; the detent is what makes a
lever that stays put usable for hovering, which a helicopter does most.

**Alternatives rejected**:
- *A detent that stops the lever until the key is pressed again*: it needs a press's edge, which
  the flight model does not see, and a held key would stop dead rather than be felt to pass.
- *Sending the lever to the server*: the server needs nothing from it (it bounds moves by the
  airframe), and other players' clients draw the aircraft as it moves.

**Verification**: `FlightTest` (the lever's rates through and past the detent and its ends; the
climb's share; let go it stays and climbs on, in the detent it hovers; the stick's tilt and spring;
a tap creeps, held it reaches top speed and the speed never passes the stick's share; held back it
brakes at once; a stick that stays flies on at its share and brakes to a hover at level; the
controls from a config's seconds). The gametests script the lever (`Rigs.level`: the hands off the
collective in its detent) where a script hovers; `letGoTheLeverClimbsOnAndInItsDetentItHoldsItsHeight`.
The booth flies the box helicopter on real keys: the lever in its detent on boarding and again on
boarding after it was left at the bottom, at the top and climbing on after Space, a Shift of
about two thirds of a second into the detent, which holds the height; the dial filmed at the top
and in the detent.
