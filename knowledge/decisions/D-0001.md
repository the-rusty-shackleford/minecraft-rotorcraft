# D-0001 — Flight

Rusty asked (2026-10-06) for a Huey and a Chinook with their own controls for going up and down
("helicopters behave differently from planes"), dyeable like the other vehicles, and that flying
around "should not harm passengers or anything but do damage the vehicles". Then he asked that
Rotorcraft be a protocol of its own rather than flight inside Vanilla Wheels.

**Decision**:

- **Layering.** An aircraft is a Vanilla Wheels vehicle (its look, seats, chests, cargo, paint,
  fuel, keys, condition, the lift) whose id is also in this protocol's synced registry
  `rotorcraft:aircraft` (`data/<ns>/rotorcraft/aircraft/<name>.json`): how it flies. This
  protocol's entity type, `Aircraft`, extends `Vehicle` and is made for those ids through Vanilla
  Wheels' `api/VehicleKinds` (its D-0030). Aircraft mods ship data only.
- **The model** (`domain/Flight`, pure, stepped on whichever side flies it: the pilot's client,
  Vanilla Wheels' boat rule, or the server with no player at the controls):
  - the rotor spools up a `spool_ticks`th a tick and climbs only at 0.9;
  - the collective aims the vertical speed at the climb, the descent or a hover;
  - the stick drives it along its heading and, let go, brakes it to a hover;
  - the pedals turn it, the velocity turning with it;
  - unpowered in the air it autorotates at 0.25 b/t, steering and gliding at 60%;
  - the body tilts nose-down with speed and acceleration and banks into turns, drawn through
    Vanilla Wheels' pose.
- **Never a hard landing.** The descent is capped by height at the sink from which it can still
  slow to 0.15 b/t before the floor, braking at four fifths of its vertical acceleration (the rest
  is margin for the step's one-tick lag): `sqrt(0.15^2 + 2 * 0.8 * a * clearance)`. Held down from
  any height, it lands at 0.2 b/t or less (JUnit from 0.5 to 300 blocks; a gametest from 30). With
  a load hanging, the floor under the load counts, read a tick's sink lower since the load follows
  after the step.
- **Nobody aboard is hurt.**
  - Its fall distance is reset every tick and `causeFallDamage` hands riders nothing.
  - A wreck in the air is not destroyed: its engine dead (`hasFuel` needs condition), it
    autorotates down and becomes a packed wreck on touchdown, riders with it.
  - Riders get out with the get-out key within three blocks of the floor, never higher.
  - It settles on water and lava: the clearance probe and the drawn pose read a fluid's surface
    as floor, so nobody goes under.
  - It runs nothing over and breaks no fragile blocks.
- **Crashes wear it** (`Wear`). A collision costs the speed carried along the direction the move
  lost: a crash past 0.25 b/t into a wall or a roof, a touchdown past 0.3 into a floor, growing
  with the square to a wreck at 1.5 b/t. On the server it is judged from the moves it validates:
  its own, or each move the pilot's client reports, compared with the one before. An impact is
  charged once; met early in a tick, the rest of it the next tick is not charged again. After an
  impact the velocity keeps only what does not drive into what stopped it (`Flight.struck`). The
  pilot's client never reports damage.
- **Keys**: Immersive Aircraft's defaults, which the friends already fly with: Space climbs,
  Left Shift descends, R gets out, G hooks, V sprays. They live in their own conflict context,
  aboard an aircraft. A mixin on `Player.wantsToStopRiding` (as IA has) keeps Shift from
  dismounting anyone from an aircraft, and one on `updatePlayerPose` keeps a rider holding it from
  crouching, which would drop the eye-anchored seat.
- **The hull** (added 2026-10-07 for the Huey, whose origin is under its mast and whose tail is
  nine metres behind it). In the air the body meets the world as its profile's `hull`: boxes
  (mesh units), each probed at points no more than 0.9 apart over its whole volume
  (`domain/Hull`), so no full block can stand inside a box unmet; a move is clamped short of any
  point entering a block's collision bounds. Without a hull, Vanilla Wheels' nose and tail points
  at half the body's length either side of the origin, as before. The rotors' `radius` and the
  hull's boxes set how far the aircraft reaches, and it is drawn whenever that box is in view.
- **No anti-cheat change.** Vanilla kicks a floating vehicle only if its gravity is at least
  1e-5, and `Vehicle` inherits `getDefaultGravity() == 0` (read in 21.1.248's
  `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.getMaximumFlyingTicks`).

**Why**: Vanilla Wheels' vehicle carries everything an aircraft needs but motion. The model is an
arcade one because the friends fly for fun; the cap makes "never hurt by flying" a property of the
model, not of luck.

**Alternatives rejected**:
- *Flight inside Vanilla Wheels*: Rusty's call, above.
- *A plane-like model* (pitch to climb): not a helicopter.
- *Shift to dismount and Left Control to descend*: no mixin, but unlike Immersive Aircraft.
- *Trusting the client's crash reports*: the server judges what it validates.

**Verification**:
- JUnit: `FlightTest`, `WearTest`.
- JUnit `HullTest`: a small box is its corners; a long one leaves no block-sized gap; reach.
- `FlightGameTests` (13): the box boom's boom comes down onto a pillar only its hull is over and
  stops there; a blade's tip four blocks out and the boom's end are inside the box it is drawn
  by; spool then climb; hover hold ±0.1; held down from 30 lands softly,
  rider unhurt; out of fuel autorotates down, unhurt; a wall at top speed costs exactly
  `Wear.crash(1.0)`, once; worn out in the air comes down before it is a wreck; reported moves
  judged once an impact; Shift keeps a rider aboard and the get-out key works only near the
  ground; a low pass runs no cow over; it settles on a pond, nobody under.
- `LiftGameTests`: built from chassis and engine, painted on the deck.
- Each rule was run against its mutation and caught it: no cap, riders handed the fall, no
  mixin, the wreck immediate, run-over on; the hull ignored (the boom went 1.96 into the
  pillar), the rotors left out of the reach.
- An aircraft-side stop at a water surface was found redundant (the flight already reads the
  surface) and removed.

**Amended 2026-10-07 (1.1.0)**: the hull's probing, the crash judging, the keys (Space, Left Shift,
R) with the get-out payload, and both mixins moved into Vanilla Wheels 1.13.0, its D-0031, when the
submarines needed them too (Rusty's call). `Wear` is Vanilla Wheels' `Crash`; this protocol's
speeds are `Flight.CRASH` (0.25, 0.3, 1.5). The rules above are unchanged, and so is every test
named here.
