# D-0002 — The sling

Rusty asked that both helicopters "drag (similarly to the trailer) a new trailer-esque item that
you load up on the ground and then hook onto the helicopter for towing", carrying animals and
chests (his call).

**Decision**:

- **What a load is.** A Vanilla Wheels vehicle with no engine and no seats whose id is in
  `rotorcraft:sling_load` (`lift`: its eye; `rope`: blocks). This protocol's `SlungLoad` makes it.
  Its chests and its cargo behind its doors are Vanilla Wheels'.
- **The rope** (`domain/Sling`, position-based): each tick the eye falls under gravity, slows by
  2% drag, and is put back on the rope's sphere if it went past. It swings, damps, follows, and on
  a slack rope just sits. It is stepped where the tow is (whoever flies the aircraft, and the
  server), through Vanilla Wheels' own towing: the link, its UUID save, the pass clock,
  `sameTrain`, the lights, recall, the wrench row.
- **Hooking.**
  - The hook key takes the nearest free load whose eye is within 1.5 blocks of straight below the
    hook and no more than the rope and 1.5 below it.
  - It lets go only when the load rests (on the ground, within a block of it, or on a fluid's
    surface); hanging, it says "Set the load down first".
  - A ground player's crouching empty-handed click on a resting load's eye lets go too.
- **Down, the load is a floor for the hook** (2026-10-07). A hooked load is of its aircraft's
  train, so Vanilla Wheels lets the two pass through each other: a pilot still holding Shift after
  setting a load down sank through it (the Huey landed through the Sling Container). Once the load
  is down on its floor (`landed`: the ground's collision, or a floor within a hair -- not
  `resting`'s "within a block", which switched too early and wore a crate setting it down at
  0.31), the descent is eased onto the load's eye and the hook stops a quarter block over it.
  Let go, the load is an ordinary vehicle again and the body meets it as one.
- **Nothing aboard is hurt.** While a load hangs its fall distance is reset every tick. Without
  this the game counted 14.2 blocks of "fall" over one carry and wrecked the crate on a soft
  set-down; a direct assertion now pins it. A load set down on water rests on the surface.
- **What wears a load.**
  - A collision costs the speed the load carried along the direction it was stopped. Not the
    rope's demand: held under a roof, the rope asks more of it every tick its aircraft climbs, but
    it carries nothing into the roof once stopped. A snagged crate took four growing charges and a
    wreck before this rule, and one charge of 41 after it.
  - A load held more than the rope and 1.5 from its hook lets go: caught under an overhang. Against
    a straight wall the short rope pulls it up the face and over; a gametest showed that first.

**Alternatives rejected**:
- *A drawbar like the trailer's*: a helicopter's load hangs.
- *Automatic catching as a trailer's tongue does*: a pilot landing beside a load would take it.
- *Charging what the rope demanded*: it wrecks a stuck load in four ticks.

**Verification**:
- JUnit: `SlingTest`, `WearTest`.
- `SlingGameTests` (5):
  - reach in and out;
  - a crate with a cow and ten apples hooked from a hover, carried east, set down from ten blocks
    up at no more than the safe sink, held down four seconds more with the hook stopping a quarter
    block over the eye and the aircraft unworn, let go only once resting, the cow and apples
    intact, the crate unworn and never counted as falling;
  - caught under a roof, it lets go and is worn;
  - the hook survives a save;
  - set down on a pond, it rests on the surface, the cow never under.
- Each rule was run against its mutation and caught it: release anywhere, no load-aware cap, no
  water stop for loads, no snag, no fall reset, no stop over a load that is down.
